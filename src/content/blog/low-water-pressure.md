Low water pressure is one of those things that creeps up on you. One day the shower's fine. A few months later it's a trickle. You don't notice because it happens gradually — until you compare it to somewhere else and realise it's bad.

Here's what causes it, what you can fix yourself, and when to call a plumber.

## The common causes

**Mineral buildup in pipes or fixtures.** If you live in a hard water area — and most of Melbourne is — minerals settle inside your pipes over time. The effect is slow: pressure drops a bit each year. Eventually you notice. This is most obvious at the showerhead and taps, where the small holes get clogged.

**Partial blockage somewhere in the plumbing.** A blocked pipe, a collapsed flexible hose, a sediment-filled valve — something's restricting flow. This can happen anywhere in your plumbing, which is why it can be tricky to diagnose.

**A leak.** Even a small leak steals pressure. Water going somewhere it shouldn't means less water getting where you want it. If your water bill's gone up and your pressure's dropped, think leak.

**Failing pressure regulator.** Your house has a pressure regulator — a valve that keeps the pressure from the street from blowing out your pipes. If it's worn out, pressure drops. You'll notice it across every tap.

**Municipal supply issues.** Sometimes it's not your plumbing at all. A burst main down the street, maintenance work, high demand — the water authority's problem becomes your problem. Check with your neighbours.

## What you can fix yourself

**One fixture with low pressure?** It's probably the fixture. Unscrew the aerator from a tap, soak it in vinegar for an hour, scrub the holes with an old toothbrush, screw it back. Same for the showerhead. Cost: $0. Time: 20 minutes. Fixes it 9 times out of 10.

**Whole house with low pressure and old pipes?** Not a DIY job. Old galvanised pipes rust from the inside out. The rust builds up and restricts flow. The fix is repiping — replacing the old pipes with new copper or PEX. That's a plumber job, and it's not cheap, but it's the only fix.

**Whole house with low pressure and recent plumbing work?** Something's partially closed. The isolation valve on the mains, the valve on the water heater, a valve someone shut and forgot to open again. Check them. And if you've had plumbing work done recently, call the plumber — it might be something they missed.

## What a plumber does

First we figure out where the problem is. Whole house or one fixture? Hot water or cold? Gradual or sudden? That tells us where to look.

Then we test pressure at different points: the main line, after the regulator, at a few fixtures. That tells us if the problem's in the street, in your house, or in a specific fixture.

Then we fix what's broken. Could be cleaning a fixture, replacing a regulator, clearing a blockage, or repairing a leak. Depends on what we find.

## What it costs

- **Diagnostic visit:** $100–$200. We come out, we figure out what's wrong, we tell you what it'll cost to fix. If you go ahead with the fix, the diagnostic fee is deducted from the final bill.
- **Pressure regulator replacement:** $200–$400. The part's cheap. The labour's the cost — shutting off the water, cutting into the line, testing.
- **Pipe work:** $500–$3,000+ depending on what needs doing. A section of pipe replaced is a few hours' labour plus parts. A full repipe is thousands.
- **Leak repair:** $200–$1,500+ depending on where the leak is and how much damage there is.

These are ballparks. We'll give you a real number after we've looked at it.
