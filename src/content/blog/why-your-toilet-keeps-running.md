A running toilet is one of those problems that's easy to ignore until you see the water bill. Then it becomes a problem you can't ignore. The good news: most of the time it's not a $1,000 plumbing emergency — it's a $3 flapper that's gone hard, or a fill valve that's got a bit of grit in it.

Here's how to figure out which one it is, what you can safely try yourself, and when it's worth calling a plumber.

## First: figure out what's happening

There are three tell-tale signs:

**The toilet runs continuously.** You can hear water flowing into the bowl all the time. This is usually a worn flapper that's not sealing, or a fill valve that's not shutting off.

**The toilet does a "phantom flush."** You hear the cistern refill briefly every 10–15 minutes, but nobody's touched the handle. This is a slow leak past the flapper — the cistern level drops, the fill valve kicks in, and it refills. Same fix as a running toilet.

**The toilet fills slowly.** You flush and it takes forever to refill. This is usually the fill valve being clogged with grit or mineral buildup, or the isolation valve (the small tap on the pipe feeding the cistern) being partially closed.

Pop the cistern lid and have a look while it's running. What do you see?

## If the water's running continuously

If water's flowing into the bowl all the time, the flapper's not sealing on the valve seat. Take the lid off and look at the flapper — the rubber disc at the bottom. If it's warped, cracked, or coated in mineral buildup, it won't seal.

The quick test: press down on the flapper with your hand. If the running stops, the flapper's your problem. Replace it.

**To replace a flapper:**

1. Shut the isolation valve (the small tap on the pipe feeding the cistern). If there's no isolation valve, shut the mains off.
2. Flush the toilet to drain the cistern.
3. Unhook the old flapper from the overflow tube and the chain from the flush handle.
4. Wipe the valve seat clean — mineral buildup there is why a new flapper might not seal either.
5. Hook the new flapper on, reconnect the chain. Leave about 1cm of slack — too tight and the flapper won't seat.
6. Turn the water back on, let it fill, and test.

Total cost: about $20 for a universal flapper kit from a hardware store. Total time: 15–30 minutes.

If the flapper's fine and the water's still running, it's the fill valve. Same process — shut the water off, drain the cistern, replace the fill valve. Fill valves are $15–$40 depending on the model. A plumber will do this for $149–$189 all-in.

## If the toilet does a phantom flush

This is the same problem as a running toilet — water leaking past the flapper — but slower. The cistern level drops a bit, the fill valve kicks in, and it refills. You hear it as a brief "whoosh" every 10–15 minutes.

Same fix: replace the flapper. If the flapper's new and it's still doing it, check the chain length. Too much slack and the flapper doesn't lift fully when you flush. Too little and it doesn't seat properly. About 1cm of slack is right.

## If the toilet fills slowly

If the cistern takes forever to refill but doesn't run continuously, the fill valve's clogged or the isolation valve is partially closed.

Check the isolation valve first — it's the small tap on the pipe going into the cistern. Turn it — if it was partially closed, full-open it and see if the fill speed improves.

If the isolation valve is fully open and it's still slow, the fill valve's clogged with grit or mineral buildup. You can try taking it apart and cleaning it, but a new fill valve costs $15–$40 and takes 20 minutes to swap. Unless you're confident taking things apart, just replace it.

## When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if:

- The cistern is cracked. A cracked cistern leaks water into the bowl and onto the floor. You need a new cistern or a new toilet.
- The toilet's leaking at the base. Water pooling around the base of the toilet is usually a failed wax seal or a cracked pan. That's a bigger job — $249–$650 depending on what's wrong.
- You've replaced the flapper and fill valve and it's still running. Could be a problem with the sewer line or a blocked vent pipe.
